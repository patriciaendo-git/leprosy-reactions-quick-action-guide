1

How to recognise leprosy reactions

Reactions are sudden inflammation caused by the immune system attacking leprosy bacteria. They are the main cause of nerve damage and disability in leprosy.

1.1Who is at risk?

RiskPerson with
HighMB leprosy with thickened nerves or nerve function impairment (NFI)
HighSkin patches near a nerve trunk or around the eye
HighHormonal change: puberty, pregnancy, childbirth
HighBacteriological index above 3+
MediumPhysical, psychological or behavioural stress
LowerPB leprosy: 1–5 skin patches, no nerve enlargement
Remember

Reactions can happen before diagnosis, during MDT and years after MDT. Most happen in the first year; in MB leprosy they can come several years after treatment ends.

1.2At every visit, check three places

Skin

  • Patches newly red, swollen, warm or tender
  • New tender nodules
  • Swelling of hands and feet

Nerves

  • Pain, burning or pins and needles
  • Tender or enlarged nerve
  • New loss of feeling or strength

Eyes

  • Pain, redness, watering, light sensitivity
  • Recent loss of vision
  • Reduced blink or a gap when closing the eyelids

1.3Check nerve function

Do it at diagnosis and at every routine visit, and at least every 3 months during MDT. Record on the NFA form (Annex A) and compare with the last visit.

Step 1 · Feel the nerves

Use the pads of your index and middle fingers. Record N normal, E enlarged or T tender. Watch the face for pain; don’t ask.

  • Ulnar: groove on the inner elbow, elbow bent to 110°
  • Median: just above the wrist, between the tendons
  • Common peroneal: outer leg, behind the head of the fibula
  • Posterior tibial: behind and below the inner ankle bone

Step 2 · Test feeling

4 sites on each palm and sole: 16 sites. The person’s eyes are closed.

  • Best: monofilament, 2 g for the hand, 10 g for the foot
  • Otherwise: a ballpoint pen, using only its own weight
  • Record ✓ felt or ✗ not felt at each site

Sensory loss = not felt at 1 or more sites.

Step 3 · Test muscle strength

NerveTestRecord
FacialClose eyes gently, then tightly; look for a gap between the lidsGap in mm (gentle and forced)
UlnarLittle finger out against resistanceS strong · W weak · P paralysed, or MRC 0–5
MedianThumb up against resistanceS · W · P, or MRC 0–5
Common peronealFoot up against resistanceS · W · P, or MRC 0–5

Step 4 · Compare with the last visit

There is new nerve damage if a nerve is newly enlarged, painful or tender; more sites are not felt; a muscle is weaker; or there is a new or wider eyelid gap.

Urgent action

New loss of nerve function, even with no pain and no inflamed skin

  1. This is a leprosy reaction. Treat now.
  2. Start prednisolone without delay.
  3. Record findings and how long the NFI has been there.
  4. Full course if NFI started less than [6 / 12 months or 52 weeks] ago to confirm
  5. Refer if you suspect complications or are unsure about steroids.

1.4Type 1 or Type 2?

Treating the nerve damage matters more than naming the type: it is treated much the same way in both.

How to manage Type 1 reaction

More immune activity against leprosy bacilli inflames the skin and nerves.

Mild

Red, swollen, warm patches. No nerve pain, tenderness or new NFI.

Local clinic

Severe

Tender nerve, new loss of feeling or strength, or acutely inflamed patches, especially on the face.

Local clinic Refer if complications

What to do · Mild
  1. Paracetamol 500 mg twice daily or ibuprofen 400 mg three times daily.
  2. Reduce weekly or every 2 weeks, by response.
  3. Check nerves and skin every visit; start steroids if nerves become involved.
  4. Continue MDT.
  5. Ask the person to come back at once if nerve pain, numbness or weakness starts.
What to do · Severe
  1. Start prednisolone now; do not wait.
  2. Complete the pre-steroid checklist first.
  3. Treat other conditions before or alongside (Annexes C and D).
  4. Treat worms: mebendazole 100 mg twice daily for 3 days.
  5. Use the standard schedule (dose finder).
  6. Continue MDT throughout.

Pre-steroid checklist

A · Does the person need steroids?

  • Loss of feeling in hands or feet
  • Weakness: eye closure, little finger, thumb, foot
  • Tender or painful nerve; reacting face patch

Any YES and recent (duration to confirm) → start steroids.

B · Special management

  • Pregnancy or child (age to confirm) → refer
  • Diabetes → monitor glucose; refer if needed
  • High blood pressure → monitor throughout
  • Cough or fever 3 weeks or more → check for TB first
  • Infected ulcer or bone infection → surgical review first
  • Mental illness → involve a specialist
  • Fungal skin infection → luliconazole cream alongside
  • Scabies → treat the household first

Specific situations

Urgent · Worse during steroids
  1. Do not stop steroids suddenly.
  2. Consider a higher dose, or keep the current dose for longer.
  3. Refer for specialist advice; keep the same dose meanwhile.
Emergency · Stopped high-dose steroids suddenly
  1. Look for low blood pressure, severe weakness, shock.
  2. Restart steroids at once.
  3. Give IV fluids if needed and admit to hospital.
Refer · Lagophthalmos
  1. Measure the lid gap in mm, gentle and forced closure.
  2. Teach eye protection: lubrication, glasses.
  3. Start or continue steroids.
  4. Refer to an eye specialist. See LG4.

Follow-up

  • Every 2 weeks while above 20 mg; monthly below 20 mg
  • Each visit: side effects, blood pressure, glucose if possible, nerve function
  • After the course: review at 3 and 6 months
  • Warn that reactions and NFI can recur after a full course

How to manage Type 2 reaction (ENL)

A whole-body immune complex disease that can recur over months or years. People feel very unwell and need treatment, reassurance and hope: ENL eventually stops.

Score severity with the ENLIST ENL Severity Scale (Annex E): 10 items, 0–3 points each, maximum 30. Mild / moderate cut-off to confirm

MildModerateSevere
SkinFew red tender nodulesCrops of painful nodules, low feverPustular, ulcerated or necrotic nodules; high fever
NervesNonePossibleAcute severe neuritis
BodyMild joint pain, malaiseSwollen hands and feet; organs possibly involvedLymph nodes, testes, organs involved
EyesNonePossibleIridocyclitis or scleritis
WhereLocal clinicReferral centreReferral centre
What to do · Mild ENL
  1. Paracetamol 500 mg twice daily or ibuprofen 400 mg three times daily.
  2. Reduce weekly or every 2 weeks, by response.
  3. ENL often recurs: re-examine nerves, eyes and general health every visit.
  4. Refer if it gets worse.
Refer · Moderate or severe ENL
  1. Refer to a referral or tertiary centre.
  2. Give painkillers while arranging referral.
  3. Send the ENLIST score and current medicines.
  4. The centre may give prednisolone 30–40 mg (moderate) or 40–60 mg (severe), clofazimine, or thalidomide.
Urgent · Red, painful or light-sensitive eye

Refer immediately to an eye specialist, whatever else you find. It may be iridocyclitis or scleritis; both threaten sight.

How to manage complications and mental health

People with active reactions are at high risk of permanent damage, and of distress.

Prevent disability

  • Teach self-care for numb hands and feet
  • Splint the limb to rest an inflamed nerve
  • Gentle active exercise as pain eases
  • Passive exercise to keep joints moving
  • Details in LG4

Messages for every person

  • A reaction does not mean MDT has failed
  • Reactions can be treated; ENL eventually stops
  • Come back at once if it gets worse
  • Involve family; consider peer support

Watch for distress

  • Anxiety about disability
  • Loss of hope after repeated ENL
  • Withdrawal, job loss, stigma
  • Psychosis or steroid dependence → refer
What to do · Anxiety, depression or loss of hope
  1. Listen without judgement; acknowledge the distress.
  2. Give clear information about the condition and outlook.
  3. Encourage family and community support.
  4. Offer stress management, problem-solving or peer groups.
  5. Refer to a psychologist, counsellor or psychiatrist if severe; link to social support.
5

How to manage neuropathic pain

A long-term result of nerve damage, often after MDT is finished.

Neuropathic pain

  • Tingling, burning, shooting or pricking pain
  • No nerve tenderness
  • No new NFI
  • Often after MDT

Leprosy reaction

  • Tender nerve
  • New or worse loss of feeling or strength
  • Inflamed patches or ENL nodules
  • Before, during or after MDT
What to do · Pain without tenderness or new NFI
  1. This is likely neuropathic pain, not a new reaction.
  2. Do not give steroids.
  3. Paracetamol, then ibuprofen, then add a neuropathic medicine.
  4. Review and adjust at follow-up.

When to refer
  • Severe Type 1 with complications or reasons not to give steroids
  • All moderate and severe Type 2 (ENL)
  • Pregnancy; children (age to confirm)
  • Infected ulcers or bone infection
  • Any eye involvement in Type 2
  • ENL not controlled, or steroid-dependent
  • Severe mental illness or psychosis
  • NFI getting worse on steroids: keep the dose and refer
Tell every person on steroids
  • Take it every day, with breakfast
  • Never stop suddenly
  • Come back at once if pain, numbness or weakness gets worse
  • A reaction is not MDT failing: keep taking MDT
  • The course lasts 20–24 weeks; finishing it helps stop the reaction coming back
  • Know the warning signs: pain, tingling, numbness, weakness, eye problems
